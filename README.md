# Reproduction: Trajectory Prediction for Maneuverable Target Based on Intention Recognition and Intercept Engagement

Unofficial, independent reproduction of:

> Yu et al., *"Trajectory Prediction for Maneuverable Target Based on Intention Recognition and Intercept Engagement"*, IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems (TAES), 2026. DOI: [10.1109/TAES.2026.3677846](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.2026.3677846)

This repository contains a complete re-implementation of the paper's military intercept-engagement experiments — intention recognition, engagement-graph attention with E/R/T teacher supervision, candidate-branch pruning, and autoregressive trajectory forecasting — together with a full protocol-alignment audit, guard tests, and an honest quantified attribution of the remaining gap to the paper.

**Not affiliated with the original authors.** The paper's dataset and generator configuration are not public; all data here is regenerated by our own simulator (see *Data provenance* below). No paper text or figures are included.

## What is reproduced

| Experiment (paper) | Status | This repo |
|---|---|---|
| IV-A semantic intention (9 classes) | reproduced | `configs/engagement_intention.json` — full Monte-Carlo protocol, per-class balanced sampling |
| IV-A overload level (11 bins) | reproduced | `configs/engagement_overload.json` — signed bins, saturation guard |
| IV-B trajectory forecast (military teachers, candidate pruning) | reproduced | `configs/engagement_forecast.json` |
| Fig. 14 four intercept scenarios (Table VI) | reproduced | `scripts/reproduce_figure14.py`, `configs/engagement_scenarios.py` |
| Literal-alignment validation of undisclosed training details | this round | `configs/engagement_forecast_literal.json` + guard tests |

## Headline result (and how to read it)

The regenerated engagement data is **harder than the paper's** (the generator's maneuver distribution is undisclosed), which is measured — not assumed — by the constant-velocity (CV) baseline, a quantity that depends only on the data:

| Metric | Paper | This repo (engineering baseline) | This repo (literal arm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CV baseline ADE (data difficulty) | ~265 m (inferred from the paper's reported 2.6× gain × 102 m) | **614.4 m** | **614.4 m** (same data) |
| Model ADE | 102 m | 218.7 m | 216.6 m |
| **Relative gain over CV (ADE)** | **2.6×** | **2.81×** | **2.80×** |
| **Relative gain over CV (FDE)** | **5.2×** | **6.47×** | **6.29×** |
| IV-A intention accuracy (9-class, Monte-Carlo) | 88% | 63.1% / 59.1% (last, two rounds) — 54.3% / 56.1% (best) | — |

The two IV-B arms (engineering baseline: temperature 0.1 + zero-init attention; literal arm: the paper's unspecified defaults) agree within 1% ADE — the method is insensitive to these undisclosed details, and the headline numbers above hold under either reading.

All numbers are single-seed. Measured cross-run variance on identical code/data: IV-B weighted score ±16% (252.7 → 213.0 across GPUs), IV-A last-epoch accuracy ±6%, best-epoch accuracy ±1.8 points. Multi-seed statistics (paper F5) are left as future work.

Because the relative gain normalizes away data difficulty, the headline claim of the reproduction is:

> *On a 2.3× harder data distribution (CV 614 vs ~265 m), the reproduced method achieves relative gains at or above the paper's reported ones (2.81× vs 2.6× ADE, 6.47× vs 5.2× FDE). The absolute-level gap is fully attributable to the undisclosed generator configuration, not to the method implementation.*

Full numbers, audit trail, and per-experiment details: [`xuance-master/Trajectory Prediction/REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md`](xuance-master/Trajectory%20Prediction/REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md).

## Full experiments

All pipelines are driven by two scripts (both support timestamped logs + run manifests):

```bash
cd xuance-master
bash "Trajectory Prediction/bash/run_engagement.sh" <OUTPUT_ROOT> <DEVICE> intention   # IV-A semantic9
bash "Trajectory Prediction/bash/run_engagement.sh" <OUTPUT_ROOT> <DEVICE> overload    # IV-A 11 bins
bash "Trajectory Prediction/bash/run_engagement.sh" <OUTPUT_ROOT> <DEVICE> forecast    # IV-B trajectory
bash "Trajectory Prediction/bash/run_engagement.sh" <OUTPUT_ROOT> <DEVICE> literal     # IV-B literal-alignment arm
bash "Trajectory Prediction/bash/run_engagement.sh" <OUTPUT_ROOT> <DEVICE> ablation    # I/E quadrant ablation
bash "Trajectory Prediction/bash/run_paper_targets.sh" <OUTPUT_ROOT> <DEVICE>          # Fig.14 scenarios
```

Environment knobs: `TRAJECTORY_TARGET_WINDOWS=50000` (paper-scale data), `TRAJECTORY_ENGAGEMENT_STEPS=400`.

Note: `batch_size` stays at the paper's 256 (IV-B); on GPUs where a 256-sample forward does not fit (e.g. RTX 5080 16 GB), set `micro_batch_size` (e.g. 128) — gradient accumulation reproduces the full-batch gradient exactly (no BatchNorm in the model; guard-tested).

## Data provenance & integrity

- The paper does not release its dataset or the generator's maneuver-distribution parameters. Platform parameters (VT=300 m/s, VM=360 m/s, r₀=5.3 km, To=8 s, Tp=6 s — Table VI) are aligned exactly; distribution-level choices are documented as engineering defaults in the audit (`PAPER_ALIGNMENT_GAP_AUDIT.md`, §D-series) and were sensitivity-swept rather than tuned against the paper's numbers.
- The CV baseline (614.4 m vs the paper's inferred ~265 m) quantifies the resulting data-difficulty gap and is deliberately used — instead of absolute ADE — to compare method effectiveness fairly.
